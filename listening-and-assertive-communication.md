# Listening and Active Communication

## Question 1: What are the steps/strategies to do Active Listening? (Minimum 6 points)

Active listening means fully focusing on the speaker, understanding what they’re saying, and showing them that you're actually listening. It helps build better understanding and reduces confusion or conflict.

The 6 strategies for active listening are:

* **Give full attention** – No distractions like phones or looking around.
* **Show you're listening** – Nodding, eye contact, and reacting naturally.
* **Don't interrupt** – Let the person finish before you speak.
* **Ask questions** – Especially if something is unclear.
* **Summarize or repeat** – To confirm what the person meant.
* **Respond properly** – Be respectful and relevant when replying.

Active listening is not just about hearing someone. It means paying attention to what they are saying, understanding their message, and responding in the right way.


## Question 2: According to Fisher's model, what are the key points of Reflective Listening? (Write in your own words, use simple English)

Reflective listening is a more detailed version of active listening. It’s about really getting the speaker’s feelings and repeating back what they said in your own words. This way, both sides are sure they understand each other.

The key points of reflective listening are:

* **Listen with empathy** – Try to understand what the other person is feeling.
* **Reflect back** – Repeat the message in your own words so the speaker knows you understood them.
* **Mirror emotions and body language** – Notice the person's tone, facial expressions, and body language so the speaker knows you understand them.
* **Confirm understanding** – Ask if what you understood is correct instead of assuming.
* **Don’t just listen to words** – Also notice the tone, emotions, and feelings behind the words.
* **Make sure both people are on the same page** – This is especially important when the conversation involves important information.

Reflective listening can be useful in many situations. For example, in business meetings, notes can be taken and then shared with the participants to check if everything is correct. In technical discussions, taking notes and confirming small details can help avoid misunderstandings later.


## Question 3: What are the obstacles in your listening process?

Some things that stop me from listening well are:

* Getting distracted, especially by my phone.
* Thinking about my reply while the other person is still talking.
* Interrupting without realizing.
* Daydreaming or zoning out during long talks.
* Assuming I already know what they’re going to say.

Sometimes, when I already have an idea about what the other person is going to say, I may stop paying full attention. Similarly, if I am thinking about my own reply, I may miss some important parts of the conversation.

These things can lead to misunderstandings, so I need to be more aware of them while listening.


## Question 4: What can you do to improve your listening?

To be a better listener, I can:

* Put my phone away when someone is talking.
* Let them finish before I respond.
* Make eye contact and nod so they know I’m listening.
* Ask questions when something is not clear.
* Summarize what I heard to check I got it right.

I can also try to stay focused during long conversations and avoid assuming that I already know what the other person is going to say. I think these small changes can help me become a better listener in both my personal and professional life.


## Question 5: When do you switch to Passive communication style in your day to day life?

I act passive when I don’t want to cause conflict or drama. Sometimes I may have something to say but choose to stay quiet because I don't want to make the situation uncomfortable.

I also become passive when:

* I feel nervous or unsure of myself.
* The other person is more dominant or confident.
* I don't want to disagree with someone.
* I am worried that expressing my opinion might upset the other person.

For example, if I have a different opinion during a group discussion but everyone else has already agreed on something, I may stay quiet instead of sharing my point.

I understand that being passive can sometimes avoid conflict, but it can also stop me from expressing what I actually think or feel.

## Question 6: When do you switch into Aggressive communication styles in your day to day life?

I get aggressive when I’m super stressed or frustrated. I may also become aggressive when I feel like no one is listening to me.

I can become aggressive when:

* I’m very stressed or frustrated.
* I feel like someone is not listening to what I am saying.
* I strongly disagree with something.
* I’m defending something I strongly believe in.
* I feel that I have explained something many times but the other person still doesn't understand.

In these situations, I may speak in a louder or harsher tone than I normally would. However, I know that aggressive communication can make the situation worse and can hurt the other person's feelings.

So, I need to learn to stay calm and explain my point respectfully, even when I am frustrated.

## Question 7: When do you switch to Passive Aggressive (sarcasm/gossiping/taunts/silent treatment and others) communication styles in your day to day life?

I notice this happens when I feel upset but don’t want to say it directly.

I may become passive-aggressive when:

* I feel upset but don't want to talk about it directly.
* I use sarcasm or jokes to hide what I really mean.
* I give silent treatment instead of talking things out.
* I make indirect comments instead of clearly explaining the problem.
* I feel hurt but don't know how to express it properly.

For example, if someone does something that upsets me, instead of directly telling them what is wrong, I might use sarcasm or stop talking to them.

I realize that this type of communication does not really solve the problem. It is better to explain how I feel directly and respectfully instead of keeping the problem inside.


## Question 8: How can you make your communication assertive? You can watch and analyse the videos, then think what would be a few steps you can apply in your own life?

After watching the videos, I learned that being assertive is the best communication style. It helps me say what I feel without hurting others.

Some steps I can take to be more assertive are:

* **Use "I" statements** like “I feel…” or “I need…” instead of blaming the other person.
* **Keep a calm and steady tone**, even during tense moments.
* **Be honest but kind** – speak my truth without being rude.
* **Prepare for tough conversations** instead of avoiding them.
* **Practice active listening** before I respond.
* **Stand up for myself** but also respect other people's opinions.

For example, instead of saying, “You are not listening to me,” I can say, “I feel that my point is not being understood. Can I explain it again?”

This way, I am clearly expressing how I feel without blaming or disrespecting the other person.

### Application in Work/Tech Context

In business or tech meetings, I should take notes and double-check them with the team or clients. During technical talks, I can paraphrase key points to confirm that I understood them correctly.

I can also use reflective listening to make sure nothing is misunderstood. When giving feedback or sharing ideas, I should stay assertive and explain my opinion respectfully.

I should also make sure everyone is on the same page, especially when small technical details matter. This can help avoid mistakes and misunderstandings while working on a project.
